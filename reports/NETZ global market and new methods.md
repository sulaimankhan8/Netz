# NETZ should sell maths-aware notes worldwide

NETZ's real market is now **learners anywhere who want handwritten maths notes and a whiteboard that work in any browser, by finger, on cheap devices**, not just Indian engineering students. That market is real but priced low. Narrow note-taking app estimates sit around **$2–3B a year growing about 9–13%**, and the leader, Goodnotes, has **25M+ monthly users** while charging only **$7–12 a year**. No product found combines what NETZ does: browser-based, touch-first handwriting with maths recognition, live solver widgets inside notes, and LaTeX/PDF export. Apple Math Notes does live maths but only on Apple devices, and MyScript converts maths but only in its apps. Two things the owner believes need correcting, respectfully and plainly. First, **AdSense does not make an age gate unnecessary.** It handles ad safety only for users Google itself identifies as minors, and it does nothing about the bigger issue: India's DPDP Act needs parental consent before NETZ stores *any* data (notes, account, analytics) of anyone under 18. Second, **the handwriting models are not free to use commercially.** All three shipped models were trained on IAM-OnDB and Google MathWriting, and both say "non-commercial". The GitHub page that hosts MathWriting's README licenses its *code* under Apache 2.0 but its *data* under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Selling worldwide is doable for a solo founder if Razorpay keeps Indian customers and a merchant of record (Dodo Payments or Paddle) handles everyone else, so that foreign VAT and sales tax become someone else's job. For new content, the strongest next build is a **linear-algebra and eigenvalue cluster** (determinant, inverse, RREF, power method). After that come the cheap Indian-syllabus wins that reuse existing engines: Milne/Adams predictor-corrector, cubic splines, difference tables with Stirling/Bessel, and Romberg.

## The corrected picture: a global notes-and-board product with free method pages as the front door

The earlier analysis treated NETZ as an exam tool for one Indian course. The owner has now clarified that **Pro and ads pay for the Notes and Board/Playground**, aimed at learners worldwide, and that testing is finished. That changes the business. The 34 method pages become the free front door that brings people in through search. The paid product is the notebook and whiteboard they stay in. Each new method also becomes a live solver widget inside Notes, so new methods add value to the paid product too.

The size numbers are fuzzy, so read them as direction, not precision. Marketintelo puts note-taking apps at **$2.1B in 2025, rising to $4.6B by 2034** ([Marketintelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/note-taking-app-market)). A broader definition from The Business Research Company reaches **$11B**, which is best treated as an upper bound ([GII/TBRC](https://gii.tw/report/tbrc1678166-note-taking-app-global-market-report.html)). Interactive whiteboards (mostly hardware) are about **$5B, and education is 52%** of that ([Straits Research](https://straitsresearch.com/report//interactive-whiteboard-market/)). Nobody sizes the exact niche of "student handwriting plus maths notes". The honest way to size it is bottom-up: Goodnotes alone has **more than 25M monthly users** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/23/goodnotes-collaborative-docs-and-ai-assitant-to-cater-to-professional-users/)), which shows how many people want handwritten notes. The device picture favours NETZ. Two free whiteboards are also going away, which gives NETZ a timely message. **Google Jamboard shut down on 31 December 2024** ([Chapman IS](https://blogs.chapman.edu/information-systems/2024/09/12/google-jamboard-is-sunsetting-on-december-31-2024/)). **Microsoft Whiteboard's standalone apps and personal-account editing are retiring in late 2026**, and legacy boards are deleted if not moved. The pitch in one line: **Apple Math Notes plus Goodnotes plus Mathpix, in any browser, by finger, for less.** The risks are that Apple Math Notes is free and improving, Goodnotes has scale and an AI push, and MyScript's recognition is mature.

## AdSense protects some minors from some ads; it does not replace an age check

The owner is partly right. Google does switch off ad personalisation and blocks sensitive ad categories (alcohol, gambling and similar) for accounts **Google identifies as minors**, including through machine-learning age estimation in the US ([Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15416897), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-begins-machine-learning-age-detection-for-ad-protections-in-us/)). AdSense also gives publishers a tool for this: the **Tag for Age Treatment (TFAT)** setting, which marks traffic as CHILD (no personalised ads or remarketing) or TEEN ([AdSense help: TFAT](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/17042704)).

That protection has three holes. First, Google's own protection only works when Google knows the user's age. A 15-year-old on a shared or signed-out browser, or outside the places where age estimation runs, gets normal ads. NETZ does know the age if it asks at sign-up, so the law expects NETZ, as the publisher, to send TFAT=CHILD or TEEN for those users. Second, AdSense requires a **Google-certified consent banner (CMP) using IAB TCF** for personalised ads to visitors in the EEA, UK and Switzerland ([AdSense help](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/13554116)). A global audience makes that mandatory, not optional. Third, and most important, **ads are only a small part of what the law covers.** India's DPDP Act defines a child as anyone **under 18**. It requires **verifiable parental consent before processing a child's data at all**, and separately bans tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted ads aimed at children ([AMLegals](https://amlegals.com/whatsapps-age-check-and-the-dpdp-act-what-section-9-means-for-childrens-data-compliance/)). Saving a 16-year-old's notebook to Supabase counts as processing, whatever AdSense does. Under the current rules these duties start around **14 May 2027** ([AMS Shardul](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)). A proposal to bring that forward to 13 November 2026 was reported ([India Briefing](https://india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-compliance-timeline-enforcement-2026-27-44740.html)), so check which date applies.

Going worldwide adds more of the same. The amended **US COPPA rule has been enforced since 22 April 2026**. It defines "mixed audience" services that must age-screen before collecting personal data and then apply COPPA to under-13s ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule)). The **UK Children's Code** covers any service "likely to be accessed by" under-18s, including providers outside the UK ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/services-covered-by-this-code/)). A learner notes app clearly falls into that category. The simplest workable design is to **ask for a date of birth at sign-up**, then choose one of two routes. (a) Allow 18+ only. That is the cheapest route, and it costs few first-year students outside India. (b) Allow minors with parental consent. Then force TFAT=CHILD/TEEN, turn off analytics profiling, and use high-privacy defaults. In India, the parent must be a verifiable adult, for example through DigiLocker ([Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/how-to-design-a-verifiable-parental-consent-process-under-the-dpdp-rules-2025)). Route (b) is the right long-term goal for a learner product, but it is real work and should get a lawyer's review. Either way, GDPR adds a privacy notice, a lawful basis for each purpose, processor agreements (Supabase, Google, the payment provider), access and deletion requests, and possibly an EU/UK representative.

## The shipped handwriting models were trained on non-commercial data

This is the claim to correct most carefully, because the evidence is in the project's own files. NETZ ships three models it trained itself from scratch: `netz_htr.onnx` (text), `netz_hmer.onnx` (maths) and `netz_router.onnx`. The training notebooks use no pretrained weights and no third-party model, which is good news: **no model licence applies, only the training-data licences.** (The earlier analysis worried about the CoMER code repo, which has no licence. That turns out not to matter, because NETZ does not use it.) The notebooks name the data plainly. The text model uses **IAM-OnDB**, and the notebook itself notes it "cannot be auto-downloaded (licence)". The maths model uses the human-written part of **Google MathWriting**, with CROHME optional. The router uses both. `NETZ_OWNER_TODO.md` already says "both datasets say non-commercial".

The "GitHub-hosted, free to use" belief most likely comes from mixing up a repository's code licence with its data licence. MathWriting's README sits in Google's `google-research` GitHub repo. It says the **code is Apache 2.0**, but the **data is "licensed by Google LLC under the Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0"** ([GitHub google-research/mathwriting](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/mathwriting)). The same split applies to the planned retrains. DeepWriting's repo is MIT, but its data is CC BY-NC-SA and extends IAM-OnDB ([ETH AIT](https://ait.ethz.ch/deepwriting)). BRUSH's data is "non-commercial research purposes" only ([GitHub brownvc](https://github.com/brownvc/decoupled-style-descriptors)). IAM-OnDB is "non-commercial research purpose only" ([FKI](https://fki.tic.heia-fr.ch/databases/iam-on-line-handwriting-database)), and CROHME is "research purpose without any commercial use" ([ISI Kolkata](https://www.isical.ac.in/~crohme/CROHME_data.html)). "Free" here means free of charge for research. The CC licence defines non-commercial as "not primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or monetary compensation" ([CC legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)). Paid plans and ads on the very Notes and Board where recognition runs make that hard to argue. Using MathWriting's synthetic part does not help, because it ships under the same licence.

There is real uncertainty. Whether trained weights legally count as "derived from" their training data is unsettled, and a lawyer could take a softer view. But a founder should not bet the product's signature feature on that. There are three clean routes, in order of speed. (1) **Email the owners for written commercial permission**: Google for MathWriting, FKI for IAM-OnDB. This is the only route that keeps the frozen maths model unchanged. (2) **Retrain the text model and router on data NETZ may use commercially.** Options are NETZ's own consented handwriting (through `/Playground/collect`, with consent wording and a privacy update), synthetic strokes NETZ renders itself, and CC BY sets such as **UJI Pen Characters** (CC BY 4.0, isolated characters) ([UCI](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/177/uji+pen+characters+version+2)) and **Quick, Draw!** (CC BY 4.0) for the router's object class ([GitHub quickdraw](https://github.com/googlecreativelab/quickdraw-dataset)). (3) Retraining the maths model is possible only if the owner lifts the freeze. No large commercially licensed online maths-handwriting dataset was found, so permission is the practical path for maths. Fix the contradiction in `HTR_FROM_SCRATCH_PLAN.md`, which still says non-commercial data is allowed. That line contradicts the owner's 21 October decision that NETZ is commercial.

## Sell abroad through a merchant of record, keep Razorpay for India

If NETZ sells abroad as the seller itself, through Razorpay International at about **3% + GST on the fee** ([Xflow](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/razorpay-international-payment-gateway-charges)), it also owns every foreign tax duty. The UK requires VAT registration **from the first sale** to a UK consumer by an overseas digital seller ([HMRC forum](https://community.hmrc.gov.uk/customerforums/vat/15f99cef-69c1-ee11-a81c-000d3a0d1900)). The EU expects destination VAT, filed through the non-Union OSS ([Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/vatlive/en/vat-news/union-vs-non-union-oss-what-is-the-difference.html)). US states apply their own economic-nexus thresholds, usually $100k or 200 sales ([Stripe guide](https://stripe.com/gb/guides/introduction-to-us-sales-tax-and-economic-nexus)). Australia, Japan, Norway and others have similar rules. One person cannot keep up with all of that. Stripe India is no help either: new accounts are **invite-only** ([Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/stripe-accounts-are-invite-only-in-india)).

A **merchant of record (MoR)** becomes the legal seller and files those taxes for you. **Paddle** charges **5% + 50¢** and includes tax filing, chargebacks and buyer support. It asks products under $10 to contact it for custom pricing, and pays out monthly, with a $15 fee if the payout is not in the local currency ([Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing), [Paddle payouts](https://paddle.com/help/manage/get-paid/is-there-a-fee-taken-for-payouts)). **Dodo Payments**, an Indian-founded MoR, charges **4% + $0.40, plus 1.5% for non-US cards and 0.5% for subscriptions**. It covers tax in 190+ countries and has built-in regional (PPP) pricing ([Dodo pricing](https://dodopayments.com/pricing), [Dodo PPP](https://dodopayments.com/billing/purchasing-power-parity)). Lemon Squeezy is being folded into Stripe Managed Payments at a similar 5% + 50¢ and has admitted slower support ([Fungies](https://fungies.io/lemon-squeezy-stripe-acquisition-saas-founders-2026/)). The fixed fee is what hurts cheap plans:

| Sale | Paddle (5% + $0.50) | Dodo (~6% + $0.40, non-US card) | Razorpay Intl (~3.5% incl. GST) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $2.99 monthly | $0.65 (22%) | $0.58 (19%) | $0.11 (4%) |
| $19.99 yearly | $1.50 (7.5%) | $1.60 (8%) | $0.71 (3.5%) |
| $39.99 lifetime | $2.50 (6%) | $2.80 (7%) | $1.42 (3.5%) |

So the practical setup is **Razorpay in INR for Indian buyers, one MoR for everyone else, and push foreign buyers to yearly or lifetime plans.** The MoR's extra 3–4 points on a yearly plan cost far less than an accountant handling foreign VAT. Confirm the current fees on each provider's own page before choosing, because these change often.

**GST export.** If NETZ itself sells to foreign buyers, the sale is a zero-rated export of services, provided all the Section 2(6) IGST conditions hold: the buyer is outside India, the place of supply is outside India, and payment arrives in foreign currency. You file a **Letter of Undertaking (LUT)** on the GST portal to charge no IGST. You keep the **FIRC/eFIRA** as proof of each foreign receipt, and invoices state "Export of services – zero rated supply" ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gst-software-saas-exports-international-clients/), [Skydo](https://www.skydo.com/blog/fira-gst-refund-saas-exporters-india)). With an MoR, your customer is the MoR, so you likely receive one foreign payout a month. Whether that payout counts as an export, and which purpose code to use, is a question for a CA. The MoR does not remove Indian income tax, GST registration or the FEMA rules on bringing foreign money home. Indian customers paying through Razorpay still get Indian GST.

## Methods to add next: one ranked list for India and the world

Two research tracks met on a short list. The global track used course syllabi from MIT, Stanford, Oxford, NPTEL, Brazil, Singapore and Turkey. It also used **12 months of English Wikipedia pageviews** ([Wikimedia Pageviews API](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/)) and solver-site keyword data. The India track used five readable syllabi (Anna MA3251, VTU BMATS201, JNTUH R22, GTU 2140706, IGNOU BCS-054) plus the GATE lists. The strongest global gaps are **eigenvalues (384k views a year)**, **determinant (313k)**, SVD, FFT and splines. "Matrix calculator" alone gets **18,100 US searches a month**, and the "with work" variant of the equation-solver search beats the plain one (14,800 vs 9,900) ([Semrush matrixcalc.org](https://sv.semrush.com/website/matrixcalc.org/overview)). That shows people want step-by-step solutions. For comparison, NETZ's mid-tier existing pages get about 95–110k (LU 95k, Simpson 108k). The strongest India gap is **Milne/Adams predictor-corrector**, which appears in Anna, VTU and BMSCE and in GATE's "multi-step methods" ([StuCor](https://stucor.in/syllabus/s-ma3251/), [ATME/VTU](https://old.atme.edu.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/BMATS201-LP.pdf), [Careers360](https://engineering.careers360.com/articles/gate-syllabus-for-mechanical-engineering)). Anna's compulsory MA3251 adds the **power method, Jacobi eigenvalues, double integration and ANOVA** for about 20 branches at once.

After these come SVD (333k, but long steps), floating-point/IEEE error analysis (192k, plus GTU and IGNOU error units), Chebyshev nodes and Runge's phenomenon, Monte Carlo integration, and conjugate gradient. Adjacent subjects draw 2–5× the interest of most numerical methods and fit the step-table format: **Taylor series (643k), Dijkstra (490k), Laplace transform (373k)**. They are worth adding once the core is done. Leave **Picard's method** (needs symbolic integration), Bairstow, Broyden and adaptive quadrature for last: they are niche or costly. Two caveats apply. Wikipedia views measure study interest, not "solver" searches, and several big Indian university syllabi (Mumbai, SPPU, AKTU, DU) could not be read. Check the top ten in Google Trends or Keyword Planner before locking the order.

One finding beats any single method. Matrix-calculator traffic is heavily Spanish-speaking: **Spain, Mexico, Colombia and Argentina send about 40% of matrixcalc.org's visits** ([Semrush](https://sv.semrush.com/website/matrixcalc.org/overview)), and planetcalc shows the same pattern ([Semrush planetcalc](https://www.semrush.com/website/planetcalc.com/overview/)). Spanish is also the clearest second language on Wikipedia for these topics. A **Spanish (then Portuguese) interface and page URLs** for the method pages and Notes likely opens more new users than any new method. Bangladesh is the other surprise: it is a top source for both GeeksforGeeks and VisuAlgo ([Semrush GfG](https://www.semrush.com/website/geeksforgeeks.org/overview/), [Semrush VisuAlgo](https://www.semrush.com/website/visualgo.net/overview/)), and it speaks to English pages priced at Tier C.

## Conclusion

Moving from "Indian exam tool" to "global notes and board" raises NETZ's ceiling, but it changes which problems are hardest. The hard problems are no longer features. They are permissions: the right to sell recognition built on research data, the right to store a teenager's notebook, and the right to collect VAT in 30 countries. Each has a cheap, known fix (an email to the dataset owners, a date-of-birth field, a merchant of record), and none should wait for more building. The less obvious lesson is that method pages are the growth engine and Notes is the till. So the best method to add is the one that both ranks in search and works as a live widget inside a notebook. The matrix and eigenvalue cluster does both. A Spanish interface may outperform all sixteen methods combined.
