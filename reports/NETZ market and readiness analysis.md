# NETZ owns a small, real niche

NETZ is aimed at a clear, steady and narrow market. **Roughly 12–18 lakh Indian engineering students sit a compulsory numerical-methods exam every year**, and the exam rewards writing out every step and table by hand. That is exactly what NETZ's 34 method pages and handwriting playground teach. Nobody else offers the same mix: exam-style step tables for the whole syllabus, handwriting input that works on Android and in the browser, code and LaTeX export, and rupee pricing. The closest rival, AtoZmath, has an old design and its owner is offering to sell it. Wolfram|Alpha costs about ₹5,800 a year. The big threat is free AI. Almost every Indian student got a free year of ChatGPT Go, Gemini Pro or Perplexity Pro in 2025, so "get an answer" is now worth ₹0 to them. NETZ wins only if it sells what chatbots do badly: exact, checkable step tables and practice for handwritten exams. The money will be modest. At realistic Indian conversion rates, 10,000 monthly users bring in about ₹20,000–40,000 a month from subscriptions and roughly as much again from ads. Treat NETZ as a lean, low-cost business that grows on search traffic, not as a venture-scale bet. The software is close to launch-ready. What actually blocks launch is the owner's own work plus three legal problems: **handwriting models trained on non-commercial data, India's data-protection rules for users under 18 (the earliest likely deadline is 13 November 2026, ten days from now), and a "no refunds" stance that consumer rules frown on.**

## Every engineering student meets numerical methods, usually in year one

India admitted **14,11,001 students to B.E./B.Tech in 2021-22** against 25.4 lakh approved seats ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/btech-engineering-college-aicte-data-course-placement-admission-graduation-aishe-vacant-seats)). The share of seats filled has since climbed from 65% to **75% in 2024-25, a five-year high**. Computer science drives the rise, while civil and mechanical shrink ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/btech-courses-aicte-engineering-enrolment-rise-ai-computer-science-cs-job-decline-civil-mechanical-data-5-yr-high-placement-salary)). Almost all of these students take a numerical-methods paper. Anna University teaches *Statistics and Numerical Methods* (MA3251) in **semester 2 to most branches** ([StuCor](https://stucor.in/syllabus/s-ma3251/)). VTU puts it in the semester-2 maths paper (21MAT21) for **every first-year student** ([InspireNignite](https://www.inspirenignite.com/vtu/21mat21-advanced-calculus-and-numerical-methods-syllabus-physics-group-2021-scheme/)). RGPV (BT-401) and GTU (2140706) teach it in semester 4 ([Career Shiksha](https://career-shiksha.com/post/syllabus-of-btech-4th-sem-aids-rgpv/), [MBIT copy of GTU syllabus](https://www.mbit.edu.in/wp-content/uploads/syllabus/UGCP/2140706.pdf)). B.Sc, BCA and MCA courses add more students, but no counts were found for them. On top of this, **7.47 lakh candidates sat GATE 2025** ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/gate-cut-off-2025-drops-electrical-aerospace-engineering-qualifying-marks-gate2025-iitr-ac-in-how-many-candidates-applied-appeared/amp)). Other sources put that figure nearer 6.9 lakh. Numerical methods is only a small part of GATE, so semester exams matter more.

Three facts about these students shape the product. First, exams test hand-written procedure, not final answers. VTU's syllabus asks only for the formulae and expects students to carry out the iterations by hand ([InspireNignite](https://www.inspirenignite.com/vtu/21mat21-advanced-calculus-and-numerical-methods-syllabus-physics-group-2021-scheme/)). Second, students search by **course code**: whole sites exist that sort notes and "important questions" under MA3251 or BT-401 ([Padeepz](https://padeepz.net/ma3251-question-bank-statistics-and-numerical-methods-regulation-2021-anna-university/), [Career Shiksha](https://career-shiksha.com/post/important-rgpv-question-bt-401-m3-iv-sem-cse/)). Third, the same textbooks come up again and again (Grewal; Jain–Iyengar–Jain), so NETZ's notation should match them. NETZ's method set (Weddle, Boole, Gauss forward/backward, chi-square and t-tests) clearly fits these Indian syllabi. Yet the repo never names a university or course code ([product audit](../research_notes/NETZ%20market%20and%20readiness%20analysis/product_current_state.md)). That gap is easy to close and worth closing.

The wider Indian edtech market gives little help. Funding fell to **$249M in 2025, down 56%** ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/lists/top-30-edtech-startups-in-india-2026-by-funding/)). Tracxn describes the sector as moving "from funding-led growth to model-led consolidation" ([Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/business-news/indias-edtech-sector-moves-from-funding-led-growth-to-model-led-consolidation-tracxn)). Published market sizes range from $2.8B to $8.9B and cannot be compared with one another, so they say little about NETZ. In plain terms, investors will not fund a growth-at-all-costs maths tool right now, and a cheap, profitable one-person product is the right shape. Several important numbers are still **unknown**: the phone vs laptop split, the Android share among these students, how much they will pay for tools, and how many people search "Newton Raphson calculator". Check Google Search Console and Google Trends before betting on any of them.

## Free AI chatbots are the real competitor, and they are weak exactly where NETZ is strong

The old answer-app business is falling apart. Microsoft Math Solver was **retired on 7 July 2025** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Math_Solver)). Photomath is being folded into Google Lens ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/02/29/photomath-google-app/)). Chegg's revenue fell **51% year on year to $51.8M in Q2 2026** ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001364954/000136495426000085/a9901-financialresultsq220.htm)), and Chegg blames generative AI and Google's AI Overviews ([SEC 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1364954/000136495426000021/chgg-20251231.htm)). The one Chegg line still growing is structured skills learning, not answer lookup. That is a useful hint about where NETZ should stand.

The substitute your users actually have is free AI. OpenAI made ChatGPT Go **free for 12 months for Indian users from 4 November 2025** ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/10/223-openai-chatgpt-go-free-indian-users/)). Google gave Indian college students a free year of AI Pro ([Google India blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/company-news/technology/students-in-india-just-got-a-gemini-upgrade/)), and Jio added 18 months free for users aged 18–25 ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/technology/reliance-teams-up-with-google-to-offer-18-month-google-ai-pro-plan-for-free-to-jio-users-3780521)). ChatGPT reportedly has **100M weekly users in India** ([tech.az](https://tech.az/en/posts/chatgpt-has-100-million-weekly-active-users-in-india-6579)). NETZ cannot compete on getting an answer at all.

It can compete on getting the right steps. A May 2026 study of ten frontier models found that at **4×4 matrices they start making up answers instead of computing them** ([LinAlg-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16675)). Multi-digit arithmetic gets worse as the work grows, and worse again when the problem arrives as a photo ([ACL Findings 2026 summary](https://tldr.takara.ai/p/2604.18203)). Those are exactly the conditions in a Gauss-Jordan elimination, a ten-row Newton-Raphson table or an RK4 run. Teachers are also pushing the same way. Indian professors are answering AI misuse with **oral quizzes and live handwritten exams** ([Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/indian-colleges-alarmed-by-ai-misuse-professors-fight-back-with-oral-quizzes-live-handwritten-exams/)). That makes practising steps by hand more valuable, not less. The pitch therefore writes itself: *"Exact, exam-format steps you can trust, and practice for the exam you have to write by hand."* A "check my AI's answer" feature would turn the chatbot's weakness into a reason to use NETZ: paste the chatbot's steps and NETZ marks the first wrong row. If you ever add an AI "explain this step" helper, the engine should still do every calculation. Small-model API calls cost roughly **₹0.02–0.12 each** on aggregator pricing ([anotherwrapper](https://anotherwrapper.com/tools/llm-pricing/gemini-2.5-flash-lite/gpt-5-nano-2025-08-07)), which is affordable if limited per user. It is not needed for launch.

Among direct tools, **AtoZmath is the closest competitor**. It is Indian and free with ads, and it covers almost the whole syllabus, including Weddle's rule, Milne and Bairstow. Its page also offers the site "with complete code" for sale ([atozmath](https://atozmath.com/Menu/ConmMenu.aspx)). That signals an owner who is losing interest, which is an opening. It is also a risk, because a buyer could revive it. Wolfram|Alpha has real numerical-methods calculators but costs **₹5,800 a year for students** in India ([App Store IN](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/id548861535)). Apple Math Notes and MyScript handle handwriting only at calculator or simple-algebra level, and Apple's version runs only on Apple devices ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/2362395/ipados-18-calculator-math-notes-apple-pencil-handwriting-equations.html), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/myscript-nebo-note-taking-for-apple-pencil/id1119601770)). Indian GATE coaching (PW, Unacademy) sells video batches at about ₹10,000. Those platforms are possible partners, not tool competitors ([PW](https://www.pw.live/gate/exams/pw-gate-ese-offer), [Unacademy](https://unacademy.com/content/gate-offer/)). No product found combines everything NETZ does. One caveat: the claim that "nobody shows Indian exam-format tables" comes from reading feature lists, not from testing each rival.

## The money is modest, and NETZ's prices sit above what the market pays

**Subscriptions.** Plan on about **1.5–3% of active users paying**. RevenueCat's 2026 median download-to-paid rate is **1.4% in India**, against 2.0% worldwide ([RevenueCat](https://revenuecat.com/report)). EdTech has the lowest freemium conversion of any industry, at **2.6%** ([CrazyEgg summary of First Page Sage](https://www.crazyegg.com/blog/free-to-paid-conversion-rate/)). The market anchors student prices low. Testbook's all-exams pass sells at about **₹299–499 a month or ₹349–399 a year** after discounts ([Zoutons](https://www.zoutons.com/testbook-coupons), [GoPaisa](https://www.gopaisa.com/books-sale-offers-deals-coupons/testbook-all-exams-unlimited-tests-yearly-pass-at-75-off)). NETZ's planned Pro prices are **₹199 a month, ₹1,499 a year and ₹3,499 lifetime** ([pricing.js](../src/app/utils/pricing.js)). The yearly price is about four times what a student pays for a full exam-prep pass. A better fit is **₹49–99 a month, ₹299–499 a year, and a one-off "semester pass" sold in the weeks before exams**. The semester pass also avoids UPI AutoPay failures and the cancel prompt that each renewal triggers. Under RBI's 2026 e-mandate rules, the customer gets a notice at least 24 hours before every automatic debit ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/04/24/rbi-issues-digital-payments-e-mandate-framework-2026/)). Razorpay charges about **2% + GST** with no setup fee ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/razorpay-payment-gateway-pricing-explained/)).

As a worked example, 10,000 monthly users × 2% paying × ₹99 comes to about **₹19,800 a month**. Reaching ₹1 lakh a month from subscriptions alone needs about 50,000 monthly users.

**Ads.** Ads add less than people hope. Indian education pages earn about **₹165–415 per 1,000 pageviews** in blog benchmarks ([upgrowth](https://upgrowth.in/how-to-calculate-google-adsense-earnings-india/)), and education ranks near the bottom of AdSense niches ([Neworld Media](https://newormedia.com/blog/top-10-highest-paying-adsense-niches/)). A long solver session counts as one pageview, new sites fill ad slots poorly, and estimates of ad-blocker use in India range from 7% to 48% ([MediaNews4U](https://www.medianews4u.com/48-of-indian-consumers-use-ad-blockers-slightly-above-global-average-doubleverify/)). So 100,000 pageviews a month is realistically worth **₹5,000–25,000**. That figure is my own planning estimate, not a sourced number. AdSense often rejects new sites as "low value content" until they have history and original pages ([Google AdSense community](https://support.google.com/adsense/community-guide/241032356/how-can-you-solve-the-low-value-content-adsense-disapproval-challenge?hl=en)). It also penalises near-identical template pages ([MonetizeMore](https://www.monetizemore.com/blog/how-deal-with-valuable-inventory-templated-page-violation-adsense)). If you build course-code landing pages, each one needs a genuinely unique worked example.

**Growth.** Paid ads do not pay back at these prices. Meta's edtech **cost per purchase is quoted at ₹400–2,500** ([Codingclave](https://codingclave.com/guides/meta-ads-management-india-2026-facebook-instagram)), which exceeds what a ₹99 subscriber is worth in months. Search is the natural channel, but it is getting smaller. Ahrefs measured **58% fewer clicks** on the top result when an AI Overview appears ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/)). The defence is to lead every page with the interactive solver working on the student's own numbers, which a summary box cannot replace. Indian edtechs grew on **YouTube and Telegram** ([The Ken](https://the-ken.com/edsetgo/indian-students-are-using-edtech-just-not-the-way-you-think/), [FactorDaily](https://archive.factordaily.com/telegram-turns-go-to-platform-for-test-prep-in-india-but-has-a-piracy-problem/)). The cheapest bets are short videos of a past exam question solved step by step on NETZ, posted in the 2–4 weeks before exams, plus free Pro for student ambassadors and for YouTubers who teach M3 or numerical methods. These ideas are untested. Retention should copy what Duolingo learned: retention rises sharply between a 1-day and a 2-day streak and flattens after about day 7, so the onboarding goal is a **7-day streak** ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks)). Study demand clusters before exams, so streak freezes and "exam sprint" goals matter more for NETZ than for a daily language app. The next big spike is **GATE on 6–21 February 2027** ([Careers360](https://engineering.careers360.com/articles/gate-2027)).

The long-term models are clear. GeeksforGeeks grew from a 2009 student blog into a business with **₹66–84 crore in revenue** over more than a decade ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2022/06/100x-engineer-teacher-entrepreneur-sandeep-jain-geeksforgeeks/amp), [Startup Atlas](https://lapaasvoice.com/startup/geeksforgeeks/financials/)). Symbolab and Photomath grew as search and viral tools and were then bought ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/27/course-hero-symbolab-makes-a-rare-edtech-acquisition/amp/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/18/math-learning-app-photomath-raises-23-million-as-it-reaches-220-million-downloads)). Desmos stayed free for students and was paid for by publishers and test makers ([EdSurge](https://edsurge.com/news/2017-05-08-desmos-passes-the-smarter-balanced-test-and-hopes-to-save-math-students-100)). For NETZ, the Desmos route would mean college or coaching-institute licences and embeds. That is worth testing once there is traffic, but there is no Indian pricing data for it yet.

## The product is built; three legal problems and the owner's tasks block launch

On features, NETZ is ahead of its own roadmap. The repo holds **34 method pages** across five units with steps, graphs and tables, plus code export to six languages. Python, JS, C++ and Go output was compiled and checked: **650 of 650 checks passed** ([NETZ_LAUNCH_CHECKS.md](../NETZ_LAUNCH_CHECKS.md)). Rust and MATLAB code is generated but has not been compiled. A same-day worst-case audit fixed 15 issues, including false "converged" messages and freezes on huge step counts ([METHOD_PAGES_WORST_CASE_REPORT.md](../METHOD_PAGES_WORST_CASE_REPORT.md)). The handwriting playground runs three recognition models on the device and is built touch-first. Notes support live solver widgets, quizzes and public pages. The rewards system has 75 medals, gems, a shop and the mascot Bolt. Supabase sign-in, Razorpay billing (where only the webhook grants Pro) and the ads code are all built ([PLAYGROUND_HANDOFF.md](../PLAYGROUND_HANDOFF.md), [NETZ_LAUNCH_PLAN.md](../NETZ_LAUNCH_PLAN.md)). The gaps are elsewhere:

- **No real-device testing.** In the project's own words, "nothing has been tried on a real phone, tablet or stylus".
- **Handwriting accuracy has never been measured** on real handwriting.
- **Several browser checks were still failing** on 21 October, and the full test suite has not been re-run since the later features landed.
- **SEO basics are weak.** The meta description reads "Best PWA app in the world!", the sitemap is missing seven method pages, and the site has no custom domain ([product audit](../research_notes/NETZ%20market%20and%20readiness%20analysis/product_current_state.md)).
- **Owner-only work is unfinished.** The test-mode end-to-end purchase run, the switch to live Razorpay, the last database migration, rotating the secrets that were shared in chat, and AdSense sign-up are all still open ([NETZ_OWNER_TODO.md](../NETZ_OWNER_TODO.md)).

The legal side holds three serious problems.

**1. The handwriting models' training-data licences.** BRUSH is "non-commercial research only" ([GitHub](https://github.com/brownvc/decoupled-style-descriptors)). IAM-OnDB, which DeepWriting is built from, is likewise non-commercial ([FKI/IAM](https://fki.tic.heia-fr.ch/databases/iam-on-line-handwriting-database)). The CoMER code repository has no licence file at all, so legally nothing is granted ([GitHub](https://github.com/Green-Wood/CoMER)). The project's own docs say the shipped text model was trained on IAM-OnDB and the maths model on MathWriting, which is CC BY-NC-SA (non-commercial). The docs also note "this conflicts with ads and paid tiers" ([PLAYGROUND_AND_HANDWRITING_IMPROVEMENT_PLAN.md](../PLAYGROUND_AND_HANDWRITING_IMPROVEMENT_PLAN.md)). The decision on 25 October 2026 to treat NETZ as "educational" for licence purposes does not hold up while the app takes subscriptions and shows ads. This risk covers the models **already shipped**, not only the planned retrain. There are three ways out. You can ask the dataset owners for commercial permission. You can retrain on data that allows commercial use or on your own synthetic data. Or you can keep recognition strictly in an ad-free, never-charged part of the product, though even that is a grey area a lawyer should confirm.

**2. Children's data under the DPDP Act.** The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 November 2025. Most duties apply from **13 May 2027**, but the government has proposed pulling the deadline in to **13 November 2026**, and some trackers already tie parental consent to that date ([India Briefing](https://india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-compliance-timeline-enforcement-2026-27-44740.html), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1773554/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs)). Section 9 treats anyone **under 18 as a child**. It requires verifiable parental consent and bans tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted ads aimed at children, with fines of **up to ₹200 crore** ([CyberPeace](https://cyberpeace.org/resources/blogs/prohibition-of-behavioral-tracking-and-targeted-advertising-for-children-under-the-dpdp-act-2023)). Some first-year students are 17. The cheapest fix is an **18+ age gate** at sign-up. The alternative is a parental-consent flow with all ads and analytics set to non-personalised for minors (`npa=1`) ([Google](https://developers.google.com/interactive-media-ads/docs/sdks/html5/client-side/consent)). Either way you also need an itemised privacy notice, a grievance contact, self-service account deletion, and a breach plan: a 72-hour report to the Data Protection Board and 6 hours for CERT-In ([AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)).

**3. Refunds and cancellation.** The CCPA's 2023 dark-patterns guidelines ban **"subscription traps"** such as hard-to-find cancellation ([Nishith Desai](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/technology-law-analysis/dark-patterns-decoded-indias-legal-and-regulatory-guide-15590)). NETZ already keeps Pro until the paid date and refunds double charges, which is close to compliant. The Refund page should say "no refunds for change of mind" and list the exceptions, rather than a flat "no refunds". The project docs also disagree with each other on refunds: one place promises a 7-day refund and another says no refunds. Fix that.

The rest is manageable. An individual can join Razorpay as a sole proprietor using a PAN, address proof, bank proof and video KYC. Since 1 January 2026, Razorpay no longer checks policy pages at onboarding ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/faqs.md)). For GST, an Indian supplier of inter-state services need not register until all-India turnover reaches **₹20 lakh** ([GSTZen](https://gstzen.in/a/integrated-tax-notification-102017-seeks-to-exempt-persons-making-inter-state-supplies-of-taxable-services-from-registration-under-section-232.html)). However, ClearTax says online education ("OIDAR") must register from day one ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/oidar-gst-registration)), so ask a CA. Gems are safe under the **Online Gaming Act**, in force since 1 May 2026 ([Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/statutory-update-new-legal-framework-for-online-gaming-from-1st-may-2026)), as long as they can never be staked, won by chance, transferred or cashed out. The Terms should say so. No Class 41 "NETZ" trademark turned up in an unofficial search ([RegisterKaro](https://www.registerkaro.in/trademark-search/net-positive-7904102)). An official IP India search, then filing in Classes 9, 41 and 42 at about **₹4,500 per class** with Udyam ([RegisterKaro](https://www.registerkaro.in/post/trademark-registration-for-msmes-and-startups)), protects the name. One more thing to check: Vercel's free Hobby plan may not allow commercial use. Confirm before you take money on it.

## Conclusion

The market research changes what NETZ should be. It is not a general "maths operating system" competing with ChatGPT. It is **the trustworthy, exam-format practice tool for one compulsory Indian course that more than ten lakh students fail or fear every year**, and it is cheap enough to run on almost nothing. The width of the product (whiteboard, notes, six-language code export, 187 shapes) is impressive, but students will arrive through method pages and course-code searches, so those pages deserve the polish. The biggest hidden risk is not a competitor. It is the licence status of the handwriting models, which the product's most distinctive feature depends on. The biggest hidden opportunity is AtoZmath's slow fade, which leaves the "numerical methods calculator with steps" searches open to a modern, mobile-first replacement.

The numbers point to cash discipline over scale. Expect a few tens of thousands of rupees a month at 10,000 users. Price at Testbook levels, not Wolfram levels. Time every push to the semester and GATE calendar. Measure before building more: real-device tests, real handwriting accuracy, Search Console impressions and the free-to-paid rate in the first exam season will tell you more than any further feature.
