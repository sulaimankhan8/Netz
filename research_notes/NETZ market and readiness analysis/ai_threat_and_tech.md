## 2. How accurate LLMs are at multi-step numerical computation

### Takeaway
Recent studies (2025–26) show LLM arithmetic gets less reliable as the number of operations and the size of the matrices grow. One study found models start fabricating answers instead of computing them at about 4×4 matrices. These are exactly the conditions in NETZ's content: iteration tables, Gaussian elimination, and Runge-Kutta steps. Tool use such as a code interpreter or calculator is the standard fix. General assistants use it inconsistently, and when they do, the output is code rather than an exam-format worked table.

### Cited Findings
- **LinAlg-Bench (arXiv 2605.16675, May 2026).** The study tested 10 frontier models on linear algebra with 3×3, 4×4 and 5×5 matrices.
  - Failures change in kind at 4×4. Smaller problems fail through sign and arithmetic slips, while larger ones more often lead to fabricated answers instead of actual computation.
  - The authors attribute this to a working-memory limit, not missing knowledge. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16675)
- **Numerical kernels in scientific software (arXiv 2604.04854, Apr 2026).** LLMs did worse than a classical baseline on simple linear-algebra kernels such as matrix-vector products, where small cancellation errors build up. They did better on long arithmetic expressions and statistical formulas. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04854)
- **Multiplication across input types (ACL Findings 2026).** Multi-digit multiplication accuracy falls steeply as the arithmetic load grows, approaching zero beyond a threshold. Accuracy is worse when the same problem is given as an image or audio instead of text. — [takara.ai summary of arXiv 2604.18203](https://tldr.takara.ai/p/2604.18203)
- **Multiplication error analysis (NeurIPS 2026 submission, under review).** In small Qwen models, higher-order digit errors are mostly off-by-one carry failures. — [NeurIPS 2026 virtual](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/159205)
- **Arithmetic inside word problems (Feb 2025).** Models are highly accurate on standalone arithmetic but degrade substantially when the same computations appear inside word problems. — [arXiv 2502.08680](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08680v1)
- **Older results (labelled older).**
  - A 2024 test found GPT-4o answered about 60% of arithmetic problems correctly without a calculator. — [Somani, 2024](https://dailyink.notion.site/Measuring-the-Arithmetic-Capabilities-of-Frontier-Models-fe700448b7c04c7faf61974221390ebd)
  - The MathGLM paper (2023) reported GPT-4 at 4.3% on multi-digit multiplication. — [catalyzex](https://www.catalyzex.com/paper/gpt-can-solve-mathematical-problems-without-a)

- **Tool use as the fix (older but foundational):**
  - PAL (Program-Aided Language models, ICML 2023) has the LLM write code and lets a Python interpreter do the arithmetic. It beat much larger models on 13 maths and algorithmic tasks, because LLMs "often make logical and arithmetic mistakes" when they compute in text. — [arXiv 2211.10435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10435v2); [ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2023/poster/23864)
  - On the MATH dataset (2023), GPT-4 scored 42.2% alone, 69.7% with Code Interpreter, and 84.32% with code-based self-verification. — [arXiv 2308.07921](https://www.arxiv-vanity.com/papers/2308.07921)
  - Code-centric approaches can overlook common sense in word problems. — [MARIO, arXiv 2401.08190](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08190v2)

### Inferences
- NETZ shows an exact, reproducible 6–10 row Newton-Raphson or bisection table, or a 4×4 Gauss-Jordan elimination with every row operation. Current evidence says general LLMs are least reliable at exactly this kind of work, especially when the problem comes from a photo, which matches the image-input finding.
- The real risk for NETZ is that students do not notice the errors. LLM output looks confident and correct, and students rarely check it. A "verified by deterministic engine" message and a "check your AI's answer" feature (paste the AI's steps and NETZ highlights the first wrong row) turn the LLM's weakness into a reason to use NETZ.
- Reasoning models with code execution (ChatGPT's thinking modes, Gemini with code execution) will probably get final answers right more often. They still don't reliably produce a university-format iteration table with the right rounding or stopping criterion.

### Gaps
- No study was found that tests LLMs specifically on Runge-Kutta, Newton-Raphson iteration tables, or interpolation tables, which is the core numerical-methods syllabus.
- No quantitative study was found comparing frontier reasoning models with and without tools on multi-step numerical problems. Searching for "tool-augmented", "program-of-thought" or "code interpreter math accuracy" papers could fill this.

## 3. Solving maths from photos and handwriting (Google Lens, Gemini, ChatGPT vision, Photomath)

### Takeaway
General vision-language models (VLMs) still trail dedicated recognisers on handwritten maths.
- In 2025, a fine-tuned VLM beat zero-shot Gemini 2.5 Flash by about 24 points on HMER benchmarks.
- Earlier evaluations found GPT-4V and Gemini near zero on OCRBench's handwritten-maths subset.
- Handwriting is a bottleneck even for maths error checking: models do better when the same content is printed.
- Multimodal LLMs also do arithmetic worse from image input than from text.

### Cited Findings
- **Fine-tuned VLM vs zero-shot Gemini.** Uni-MuMER (arXiv 2505.23566, May 2025) fine-tunes an open VLM for HMER. On CROHME and HME100K it reports beating the best zero-shot VLM, Gemini 2.5 Flash, by 24.42% and the best lightweight specialist model, SSAN, by 16.31%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23566v2)
- **GPT-4V on handwritten maths (2023, older).** A quantitative OCR evaluation found GPT-4V at very low accuracy on handwritten mathematical expression recognition, far below supervised specialist models. — [arXiv 2310.16809](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.16809)
- **OCRBench summary.** A leaderboard summary of OCRBench (100-sample handwritten-maths task) describes GPT-4V and Gemini as near zero on HMER. This is a secondary description. — [alphaXiv OCRBench](https://www.alphaxiv.org/benchmarks/south-china-university-of-technology/ocrbench)
- **FERMAT (2025).** This benchmark tests error detection and correction in handwritten maths. Gemini 1.5 Pro had the best error-correction rate at 77%, and accuracy rose when the handwriting was replaced by printed text. — [alphaXiv FERMAT](https://www.alphaxiv.org/benchmarks/chennai-mathematical-institute/fermat)
- **Image input lowers arithmetic accuracy.** The same multiplication problem given as an image gets lower accuracy than when it is given as text. — [ACL Findings 2026 summary](https://tldr.takara.ai/p/2604.18203)
- **Photo feature in the Jio offer.** Google's 18-month Jio AI Pro offer bundles Gemini, which includes photo-of-homework solving. — [Smartprix](https://smartprix.com/bytes/jio-is-offering-18-months-of-gemini-pro-with-2tb-cloud-storage-for-free-heres-how-to-claim)

### Inferences
- The camera-to-answer flow is now free and built into the default tools (Gemini, Google Lens and ChatGPT on Android). NETZ's handwriting input should not try to be "snap and solve". It should be a practice surface: write your own steps, get them recognised and checked line by line.

### Gaps
- No 2026 head-to-head was found for GPT-5-class or Gemini 3-class models on handwritten university-level maths (matrices, multi-line work). The VLM figures above are from 2023–25 models, and newer frontier models are probably better.
- Background, unverified: Google acquired Photomath (completed in 2023) and folded its features into Google Lens and Gemini. This needs checking.

## 4. What students and teachers say, and how universities regulate AI

### Takeaway
The picture is mixed. Leading institutions are becoming more permissive and use GenAI for course materials. At the same time, many universities reportedly forbid AI for assignments, and professors are moving towards oral quizzes and live handwritten exams. That shift raises the value of practising handwritten step-by-step solutions, which suits NETZ.

### Cited Findings

Professors are responding with oral quizzes on submitted work and live handwritten exams. ### Inferences
- Professors are moving to live handwritten exams and oral checks. ### Gaps

- No India-specific 2025–26 survey of engineering teachers or students on AI and cheating in maths was found.

## 6. Defensible openings, and whether NETZ should use LLM APIs itself

### Takeaway
The defensible ground is what general LLMs do badly or don't do at all:
- exact, verifiable step tables in syllabus or exam format
- deterministic recomputation, such as changing x0 or the tolerance and getting a reproducible result instantly at no cost
- handwriting practice that is checked
- visualisation
- streaks and practice habits
- offline use and low data

Cheap LLM APIs could be added as an optional explanation layer on top of the deterministic engine, with the LLM never doing the arithmetic. Small models cost about $0.05–0.30 per million input tokens and $0.40–2.50 per million output tokens.

### Cited Findings
- **Small-model API prices (aggregator data, Sept 2026, check against official pages):**
  - GPT-5 nano: $0.05 input / $0.40 output per million tokens. — [anotherwrapper](https://anotherwrapper.com/tools/llm-pricing/gemini-2.5-flash-lite/gpt-5-nano-2025-08-07)
  - GPT-5.4-nano is listed at $0.20 / $1.25. This conflicts with the line above and suggests newer nano versions were repriced. — same source
  - Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite: $0.10 / $0.40.
  - Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite (around July 2026): $0.30 / $2.50. — [anotherwrapper](https://anotherwrapper.com/tools/llm-pricing/gemini-2.5-flash-lite/gemini-3.5-flash); [morphllm](https://www.morphllm.com/gemini-api-pricing)
  - Real invoices vary with caching, batch discounts and context tiers. — [cloudinsight](https://cloudinsight.cc/en/blog/gemini-api-pricing)
- **LLMs fail at scale.** LLMs fabricate answers on larger matrix computations, so they should not compute the numbers. — [LinAlg-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16675)

### Inferences
- **Cost per call.** One "explain this step" call is about 1,500 input tokens (problem, engine table and syllabus prompt) plus 400 output tokens.
  - On GPT-5 nano at $0.05/$0.40 per million: about $0.000075 + $0.00016 ≈ $0.00024, or about ₹0.02 per call.
  - On Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite at $0.30/$2.50: about $0.00145, or about ₹0.12 per call.
  - 1,000 active users × 20 calls a day × 30 days = 600K calls a month. That costs about $140 on nano or about $870 on 3.5 Flash-Lite.
  - Affordable, but it needs per-user quotas tied to gems or subscription tier. These are my own calculations from the cited prices.
- **Risks of adding an LLM:** hallucinated explanations that contradict the deterministic table (mitigate by grounding the prompt on the engine output and blocking the model from stating new numbers), dependence on vendor prices or terms, data protection for minors under India's DPDP Act, and latency on low-end phones.
- **Openings general AI leaves:**
  1. Syllabus-exact tables: AKTU, VTU, Mumbai University and similar formats, with rounding to 4 decimal places and the textbook stopping rule.
  2. A "verify an AI answer" tool.
  3. Handwriting practice where the student writes each step and NETZ checks it, rather than snap-and-solve.
  4. Interactive graphs and convergence animations.
  5. Gamified daily practice, which ChatGPT does not do.
  6. Teacher tools such as auto-generated variant problems with answer keys.
  7. Offline or PWA use with no per-query cost.

### Gaps
- Official OpenAI and Google pricing pages were not fetched. The figures come from aggregators and should be confirmed.
- No data was found on Indian students' willingness to pay for a niche maths tool. The Perplexity revenue data in section 1 is the closest proxy.

## 7. State of open-source handwritten maths recognition (HMER), and MyScript

### Takeaway
- **Classic specialist models.** CoMER (2022), TAMER and PosFormer (2024) reach about 59–62% exact-match expression recognition (ExpRate) on CROHME 2014–2019. About 4 in 10 expressions have at least one error, so a correction UI is essential.
- **2025 VLM-based models.** Fine-tuned VLMs like Uni-MuMER report much higher figures: roughly mid-70s to 85, depending on the set and whether external data is used. These are multi-billion-parameter models, too heavy for phones.
- **On-device options.** Small browser-ready formula recognisers exist: Texo at 20M parameters (runs in Transformers.js), RapidLatexOCR ONNX via onnxruntime-web, and Mini-CoMER at 6.4M parameters. MyScript is the mature commercial alternative but does not publish SDK prices; it is sold through sales contact, with a free trial.

### Cited Findings
- **TAMER (2024, AAAI 2025).** ExpRate is 61.23%, 60.26% and 61.97% on the CROHME 2014, 2016 and 2019 test sets. It reduces bracket-matching errors and compares itself with CoMER and ICAL. — [arXiv 2408.08578](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08578v2); [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/33190/35345)
- **PosFormer (ECCV 2024).** It improves on prior work by +2.03, +1.22 and +2.00 points on CROHME 2014, 2016 and 2019, +1.83 on multi-line M2E, and +4.62 on the complex MNE set. It adds no inference latency. — [arXiv 2407.07764](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07764v1); [ECCV](https://eccv2024.ecva.net/virtual/2024/poster/2218)
- **CoMER (ECCV 2022).** 59.33% ExpRate on CROHME 2014. The official code is PyTorch. — [arXiv 2207.04410](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.04410)
- **Other 2025 specialist models.** MMHMER (Feb 2025) reports 60.85% on CROHME 2014 without augmentation, and SemiHMER (Feb 2025) reports 60.95% with augmentation. Specialist-model accuracy has levelled off at about 61%. — [arXiv 2502.05557](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.05557); [arXiv 2502.07172](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.07172)
- **Uni-MuMER (May 2025).**
  - It reports CROHME results of roughly mid-70s to about 79 for the base model trained only on CROHME, and up to about 85 with external data.
  - The exact per-set split could not be read from the extracted table.
  - It also proposes ExpRate@CDM, a metric that does not penalise LaTeX that differs in syntax but looks identical. — [arXiv 2505.23566](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23566)
- **Structural GNN approach (Aug 2025).** 74.14% expression accuracy on CROHME 2023, and it also outputs a stroke label graph. — [arXiv 2508.19773](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19773v1)
- **MathWriting dataset (Google, published 2025).** A large online-ink maths dataset of human and synthetic samples, with normalised LaTeX labels. — [arXiv 2404.10690](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2404.10690)
- **Browser deployment options:**
  - Texo is a formula-recognition model under 20M parameters, deployed as a front-end-only web app with Transformers.js. — [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/es/review/texo-formula-recognition-within-20m-parameters)
  - RapidLatexOCR ONNX weights run fully in the browser via onnxruntime-web. — [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/inYourOwnBrowser/rapid-latex-ocr-onnx)
  - A GeoGebra-related ICMS 2026 paper compared onnxruntime-web backends for a small handwriting model and found WebAssembly fastest. That test used digit recognition, not maths expressions. — [ICMS 2026](https://icms-conference.org/2026/papers/paper29/main.pdf)
- **MyScript iink SDK.**
  - The SDK is at version 4.5. Version 4.4 "significantly" improved maths recognition and added maths inside handwritten tables and decimal-place control in JIIX export.
  - It can solve some equations, and maths recognition uses a neural Math Recognizer (since SDK 3.0).
  - SDK pricing is not public: contact sales, free trial available. MyScript's consumer app costs $1.99–24.99 in iOS in-app purchases. — [MyScript developer docs](https://developer.myscript.com/doc/interactive-ink/4.5/overview/about/); [idp-software](https://idp-software.com/vendors/myscript/); [MyScript blog](https://www.myscript.com/blog/iink-sdk-3-0-handwriting-recognition/)

### Inferences
- NETZ's own HMER model is frozen per project decisions. Its value lies in the practice and checking workflow, not in recognition accuracy alone, because about 60% exact-match is the open-source ceiling as of 2024. Showing the recognised result for confirmation before solving matters.
- MyScript would mean per-user or per-device licence costs of unknown size. For a low-price Indian product, an open-source ONNX model on the device or a cheap server is the economical path.

### Gaps
- No authoritative CROHME leaderboard as of 2026 was found. Papers mix test years and augmentation settings, so their numbers are not directly comparable.
- No published browser or ONNX latency figures were found for CoMER, TAMER or PosFormer. NETZ would need to benchmark onnxruntime-web (WASM vs WebGPU) on its own target devices.
- MyScript SDK pricing is still not public.
